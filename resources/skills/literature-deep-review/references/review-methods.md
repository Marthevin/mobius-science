# Review method choices

Choose a review type before searching. A focused narrative synthesis can be rigorous without claiming that every eligible paper was found. A scoping review maps a defined field and should use a documented search/screening process. A systematic review has additional protocol, screening, risk-of-bias, and reporting requirements; use the current [PRISMA 2020 guidance](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) and any field-specific extension. Do not relabel a narrative search after the fact.

Search at least one positive and one null/contradictory direction with full query strings. Add domain-appropriate sources: e.g. PubMed for biomedical research, PsycINFO for psychology when accessible, archaeology journals/repositories for cultural history, and trial registries for interventions. Mark unavailable sources. Deduplicate by DOI and by underlying cohort; multiple endpoints or follow-up papers may not be independent studies.

For every material claim, preserve the exact language of the claim, source record, quote and page, first judgement, masked second judgement, and conflicts. Do not infer causality from observational associations; distinguish primary data from a review's retelling. For graphs and tables, inspect the original visual and record the relevant panel/row. Check whether corrections or retractions affect key sources.

Write synthesis paragraphs across studies and conditions, not one paragraph per paper. Report effect direction, population, design, sample size and uncertainty when known. Explain what would change the conclusion. Use the output statuses to calibrate language, then perform a separate domain-expert assessment of bias, heterogeneity and generalizability.
