# Reasoning before polish

## Build an argument map, not a contents list

For each consequential section record: its job, the proposition it develops, the concrete material, what in that material matters, why that feature licenses the inference, the strongest alternative, and the limit. Write the central answer in ordinary language before making it more technical. Scope it to the sources actually available.

Ask whether a reader could accept the evidence yet reject the conclusion. Identify the missing warrant. Concept definitions should change what the writer can infer: if removing the theorist's name leaves the analysis unchanged, the concept may be ornamental. Several concepts can work together, but explain their compatibility and division of labor rather than stacking names.

A paragraph often needs claim, evidence, analysis and a link to the larger argument. This is a diagnostic, not a four-sentence template. Vary paragraph shape with its purpose; let readers see what changed in their understanding.

## Example: inference without invented evidence

Weak: “Students listen to music after class; therefore they resist institutional discipline.”

Better question: Does the described practice appropriate institutionally organized time or space for a use not exhausted by its prescribed purpose? Ordinary rest, deliberate opposition, accommodation and tactical appropriation are rival readings. Assess the actual context and consequences before choosing. Do not make rule-breaking or explicit political intent a universal requirement for de Certeau's tactics; check the specific theoretical passage.

When no observation exists, write this as a hypothetical analytic distinction, not a finding about a campus. When a real user observation is provided, say what it establishes and what additional observation would distinguish the readings. This example models inferential discipline; it is not a theory-specific answer to reuse in other disciplines.

## Counterargument that matters

Represent a plausible, strong alternative in terms its proponent would recognize. Test it against the same evidence. The result may be rebuttal, concession, a boundary condition, revised thesis or unresolved disagreement. A token “there are limitations” paragraph does no work if the conclusion remains absolute.

For an empirical study, alternatives may be addressed in design, robustness analysis or discussion rather than an essay-style counterargument section. Do not promise new analyses that were not performed.

## Revision order

1. Reverse-outline the actual draft: one sentence for each paragraph's job. Detect repetitions, missing steps, irrelevant background and headings that promise evidence never provided.
2. Check the main answer against the body and strongest objection. Remove unsupported causal, representative or novelty claims; changing the central claim may be the right revision.
3. Check pivotal source passages and numerical/chronological details. A citation with several clauses may support only one.
4. Revise section sequence and transitions so the reader understands why the next question follows.
5. Edit sentences for exact agents, verbs, terms and scope. Preserve meaningful nuance; remove jargon that conceals a missing explanation.
6. Re-read the ending. It should state what the analysis now permits the reader to conclude, not introduce a stronger claim or a new theory.

Save actual before/after examples and reasons in the revision memo. Do not announce that a problem was fixed when only its wording changed. One complete substantive pass and a focused verification pass are preferable to repeated global polishing without a diagnosed defect.
