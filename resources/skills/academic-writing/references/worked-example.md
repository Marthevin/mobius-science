# Worked reasoning example — synthetic, not research evidence

This miniature example illustrates a reasoning move, not a source to cite. All
people, observations and publications below are invented **for this example**.
Never transfer its numbers, citation labels or verdict into a real manuscript.

**Brief:** a short critical essay asks whether a library's informal use can be
called resistance. No interviews or personal account were supplied. Media may be
analyzed as representation. The article language is English; source languages are
unrestricted.

**Inspected synthetic packet:**

- S1, an illustrative theory excerpt, paragraph 2: “Reusing a place need not
  oppose its governing institution. Opposition is a further interpretive claim.”
- S2, an illustrative field report, page 4: “The study describes one library.
  Staff explicitly permitted conversation in the foyer; other rooms were not
  observed.”
- S3, an illustrative media text, paragraph 1: “Students are reclaiming the
  library.” This is the text's framing, not verified evidence about all students.

**Weak sentence:** “Students across universities reclaim library space as
resistance to institutional discipline.” Problems: universal population from one
case; reuse equated with opposition; a media phrase treated as an observed motive;
and no evidence about disciplinary rules or recognition.

**Better central claim:** “In this case, informal use is better explained by
institutional permission than by opposition; the language of reclamation does
additional interpretive work that the reported observation does not establish.”
This is a positive answer to the question, not only a list of unavailable data.

**Argument map:**

| Move | Evidence | Warrant | Rival/limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinguish reuse from opposition | S1 paragraph 2 | The theory explicitly leaves opposition undecided | Another theory could define resistance differently; inspect it |
| Explain the observed foyer use | S2 page 4 | Permission predicts the same behavior without an oppositional motive | Permission does not establish participants' private intentions |
| Analyze the reclamation label | S3 paragraph 1 | It is direct evidence of that publication's framing | One text is not evidence of widespread discourse or prevalence |

**Example ledger decision:** the second row is an `interpretation`, treated as
`qualified`, linked to S2's exact passage with a warrant. Its source snapshot and
manuscript hashes are computed from actual files. S3 has `source_type: media` and
`evidence_role: representation`; a scholarly-only brief would instead exclude it
and require rewriting the framing claim. An English manuscript alone would not.

**Substantive review:** contribution identifies a distinguishable alternative;
argument tests permission against the same observation; evidence anchors the
scope; precision separates reuse, permission and resistance; alternatives leave
intentions open; transparency states that this is synthetic. No amount of matching
hashes would make “all universities” defensible. A real review must cover the other
rubric dimensions on the actual complete manuscript as well.

**Conclusion:** retain the bounded answer. Do not conclude that resistance is
impossible, invent interviews to settle intent, or put the audit's tool history in
the essay. In a proposal, turn the unresolved intention/recognition question into
a feasible planned observation, with consent and an access-dependent fallback.
