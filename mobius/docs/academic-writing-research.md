# Mobius Science academic-writing skill: research and evaluation design

Research checked 2026-09-26. This is a proposed operational framework, not a validated grading instrument, a score for the user's samples, or evidence of competitive superiority. The Banwei, de Certeau/campus, and Yangshao examples are task cases, not gold-standard answers. Source guidance below is distinguished from the proposed engineering and evaluation decisions.

## Twelve verified primary or authoritative sources

1. **AAC&U, Written Communication VALUE Rubric** — [primary rubric, hosted by University of Baltimore](https://www.ubalt.edu/_migrated-binaries/ubalt.edu/institutional_effectiveness/uploads/documents/assessment_handbook/written%20communication.pdf). Evaluates audience/purpose, content, genre conventions, evidence, and language. It explicitly requires local adaptation and is designed for institutional assessment, not direct grading. **Use:** a framework for separate dimensions, not a universal academic-quality score. The AAC&U landing page returned 403; this accessible university-hosted copy was read instead.

2. **Harvard College Writing Center, Thesis** — [guide](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/thesis). Analytical theses should make a contestable, sufficiently focused claim supported by available evidence; the guide distinguishes analytical from normative claims. **Use:** check that the draft answers an actual question and that its scope fits its source base. Do not mechanically require argumentative theses for genres whose task is descriptive reporting.

3. **Harvard College Writing Center, Counterargument** — [guide](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/counterargument). Objections belong in the reasoning process, and compelling objections may require revising the thesis. **Use:** test the strongest relevant alternative explanation, not a ritual paragraph containing a weak objection.

4. **University of Wisconsin–Madison, Using a Reverse Outline to Revise** — [faculty-authored guide](https://dept.writing.wisc.edu/wac/using-a-reverse-outline-to-revise/). A paragraph-by-paragraph outline exposes unfocused paragraphs, misplaced ideas, and mismatch between promised argument and actual organization. **Use:** revise structure before polishing sentences. This archival page was directly accessible; the current handbook URL repeatedly timed out.

5. **俞志元 / Fudan University, 学术写作的要点与步骤 (2019)** — [Chinese faculty guidance](https://news.fudan.edu.cn/2019/0606/c1015a97003/page.htm). Connects claims, reasons, evidence, and the principles that make an inference work; starts from a narrowed research question and emphasizes critical reading. **Use:** an authoritative Chinese-language grounding for explicit reasoning, rather than treating Chinese academic prose as ornamental translation. This is pedagogical guidance, not an empirical validation of a rubric.

6. **American Historical Association, Statement on Standards of Professional Conduct (2023)** — [professional standards](https://www.historians.org/resource/statement-on-standards-of-professional-conduct/). Requires faithful use of sources, a retraceable evidentiary trail, acknowledgement of uncertainty, and engagement with contrary evidence. Source status depends partly on the question asked. **Use:** separate records, later interpretations, and the writer's inference; never borrow an unexamined primary citation as if inspected firsthand.

7. **Society for American Archaeology, Editorial Policy and Style Guide (December 2023)** — [official PDF](https://www.saa.org/common/Uploaded%20files/saadocs/Publications/Style%20Guide/saa-style-guide_english_updated_2021_final08023c15928949dabd02faafb269fb1c.pdf). Sections 1.0–1.1 distinguish article, technical report, comment, and forum contributions; sections 1.1.7 and 3.9 address data availability. **Use:** genre and venue determine the contribution, documentation, and length expected. This is an example of journal-specific requirements, not a submission recommendation for a Yangshao paper or a universal archaeology template.

8. **EQUATOR Network, What is a reporting guideline?** — [definition and study-type routing](https://www.equator-network.org/about-us/what-is-a-reporting-guideline/). Reporting guidelines specify minimum information for particular research designs. **Use:** select reporting standards after identifying the actual study type; do not impose a health-research checklist on a humanities essay.

9. **STROBE Initiative, Aims and use** — [official statement](https://www.strobe-statement.org/). STROBE concerns reporting cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional research. It explicitly is neither a study-design prescription nor a research-quality appraisal instrument. **Use:** maintain separate checks for reporting completeness and validity of the underlying study.

10. **ICMJE, Use of AI by Authors** — [current recommendations](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html). Humans retain responsibility for AI-assisted submissions; the recommendations require disclosure and review for errors, bias, attribution, and originality. **Use:** record assistance accurately and apply the actual course/journal policy. These are medical-journal recommendations; other venues may differ.

11. **Gao et al., Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations, EMNLP 2023** — [publisher record](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/). ALCE separates fluency, correctness, and citation quality. Its passage-based evaluation distinguishes support coverage from irrelevant citations; the paper describes limitations of automated entailment. **Use:** verify what the cited passage supports, not merely whether the publication or DOI exists. Its results concern specified 2023 models and QA datasets, not today's full-length scholarly writing.

12. **Zheng et al., Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena (2023)** — [full author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4). Finds position and verbosity effects and limitations in judging reasoning; studies possible self-preference but notes insufficient evidence for a firm causal conclusion. Section 3.4 proposes swapping answer order. **Use:** blind comparisons, order reversal, and disagreement handling; do not equate chatbot preference agreement with scholarly validity.

## Proposed operational rubric

No numerical scores or weights are justified by the supplied samples. For each applicable dimension, record **strong / adequate / revision needed / unassessable**, the exact draft passage, the reason, and a concrete revision. “Unassessable” is necessary when a source, dataset, prompt, or venue requirement is unavailable. An elegant sentence cannot offset fabricated evidence.

| Dimension | Observable strong performance | Revision trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Task, audience, and genre | Makes the requested intellectual move at the requested length and level; delivers the requested artifact | Generic essay substituted for a research report, commentary, revision, or analysis |
| Question and contribution | Identifies a bounded problem and states what the analysis adds at the appropriate level | Topic announcement, unsupported novelty claim, or thesis broader than evidence |
| Argument and warrant | Explains how particular evidence supports each consequential inference | Evidence dump, theoretical name-dropping, missing causal or interpretive bridge |
| Evidence quality and coverage | Uses relevant material, inspects pivotal passages, identifies provenance and limitations | Unverifiable quotations, selective evidence, unsupported generalization |
| Conceptual and disciplinary precision | Defines consequential terms; applies concepts through explicit criteria; respects method and scale | Treating a theory as a label, incompatible concepts, causal claims from description |
| Alternatives and uncertainty | Tests a plausible rival and lets contrary evidence constrain conclusions | Straw man, absolute certainty, obligatory but irrelevant objections |
| Structure and development | Paragraphs have distinct jobs and a necessary sequence; conclusion answers the question | Repetition, disconnected headings, conclusion stronger than body |
| Language and reader guidance | Precise, economical English or Chinese appropriate to the field; stable terminology | Ornament replacing substance, translation changing strength or meaning, mechanical transitions |
| Attribution and research transparency | Clearly marks borrowed ideas, quotation, translation, interpretation, methods, and applicable disclosures | False firsthand access, invented methods/data, unattributed borrowing |

**Anchors:** strong = sustained, specific execution with no material defect; adequate = core purpose fulfilled with localized limitations; revision needed = a material problem compromises the argument or task; unassessable = required evidence is absent. Integrity failures and wrong-task outputs are recorded separately as delivery blockers, not averaged into a pleasing overall score. User/instructor/journal requirements take precedence over this provisional framework.

## Genre distinctions

These are synthesized routing decisions from the sources, not universal publication rules.

| Genre | Appropriate ambition | Evidence and structure | Do not manufacture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Course analytical essay | A defensible interpretation demonstrating learning and answering the assignment | Close engagement with assigned materials; focused question, analysis, relevant objection | A field-wide literature gap or publishable novelty |
| Publishable research manuscript | A specified contribution assessed against a real venue and scholarly conversation | Adequate research/materials, appropriate methods, warranted results, uncertainty, required reporting and data statements | Experiments, interviews, archive access, peer-review readiness, acceptance likelihood |
| Historical/archaeological commentary or short article | A clearly signposted interpretation or synthesis for its audience | Separate observations, chronology, scholarly interpretations, and the author's claim; preserve retraceable support | Evidence of social organization from a single artifact class without a justified inferential chain |

Shortness is not a genre. A short research report, public commentary, and course essay have different evidentiary obligations. Humanities publication can contribute an interpretation or synthesis; it need not invent a quantitative hypothesis or adopt IMRaD. Likewise, an empirical paper need not insert an essay-style counterargument paragraph when alternative explanations belong in its design or discussion.

## Recommended writing process

This workflow is an implementation proposal informed by the guidance, not a claim that a controlled trial proved this precise sequence optimal.

1. **Specify the brief.** Capture task, audience, discipline, genre, language, length convention, sources allowed, citation style, deadline, and any actual rubric. Make reasonable low-risk assumptions explicit; do not fabricate missing assignment criteria.
2. **Read before committing to an answer.** Build a compact record of consequential evidence and disagreements. Narrow the question to what available material can address.
3. **Develop a provisional argument.** Map claim → evidence → warrant → alternative → limit. If the evidence fails, change the claim.
4. **Design the structure.** Give each section a job and decide what the reader must understand first. Draft the analysis around evidence.
5. **Audit the draft's actual reasoning.** Reverse-outline it, test its strongest objection, and check that the conclusion follows from the body.
6. **Check sources and facts.** Resolve consequential unsupported claims by verification, narrower wording, explicit uncertainty, or removal. Keep a source-incomplete working draft visibly distinct from a verified deliverable.
7. **Edit the target language.** Revise conceptual precision, paragraph transitions, rhythm, and economy. Then check quotations, references, counts, and venue requirements.

For EN/ZH work, keep a small term ledger recording the chosen translation, source edition, and alternatives when they matter. Preserve modality, negation, causal force, and scope across languages. Count Chinese characters versus English words according to the brief rather than assuming equivalence. These are proposed checks, not claims about fixed national writing styles.

## Citation audit beyond metadata

Maintain three distinct statuses: **identity checked**, **relevant passage inspected**, and **claim supported**. A correct title, author, year, or DOI establishes only the first. A search snippet is not full-text inspection.

For consequential claims, record the source and edition, accessible location/page/section, the bounded proposition it supports, access limitations, and whether the statement is direct evidence, paraphrase, translation, or inference. Split a sentence if one citation supports only half of it. A truthful attribution can still depend on a weak source; assess source quality separately. Never invent page numbers, make unseen primary evidence appear firsthand, or silently convert a hypothesis into a finding.

## Failure tests for the skill and its evaluator

The following are proposed adversarial fixtures, not factual findings about the user's sample topics. Attach real source packets before testing factual accuracy.

| Test fixture | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| A plausible but nonexistent reference | Flag inability to verify; never manufacture metadata |
| Real DOI, source contradicts cited claim | Fail support despite passing identity check |
| Accurate quotation, changed negation or omitted qualification nearby | Preserve context; identify the distortion |
| Abstract available, full text unavailable | State access boundary; do not claim page-level verification |
| Banwei behaviors compatible with coping, consumption, or resistance | Identify discriminating evidence and rival explanations; do not force every behavior into resistance |
| Campus vignette and de Certeau terminology | Explain the conceptual criteria and mechanism; do not let the author's name substitute for analysis |
| Yangshao observation followed by sweeping claims about kinship or civilization | Expose the inferential gap; request/use appropriate evidence or narrow the conclusion |
| One convenient case used for a population claim | Limit generalization or identify the needed sampling/comparative basis |
| User asks for a short commentary | Deliver that genre without fabricating methods or a research gap |
| Polished draft with missing research data | Improve what can be improved; preserve missing-data status |
| Chinese rewrite of a qualified English claim, and reverse direction | Preserve certainty, causality, attribution, and technical meaning |
| Strong compact paragraph versus longer repetitive rewrite | Prefer substantive quality; detect verbosity bias |
| Same two drafts with order and labels swapped | Stable preference or explicit unresolved/tie outcome |
| Unseen discipline, new topic, and new source packet | Apply principles without reproducing the three supplied samples' surface patterns |

## Credible evaluation protocol

Use the existing samples to discover task requirements and failure modes; freeze new held-out prompts before iteration. Include both languages, several genres, source-rich and source-poor requests, revision tasks, and at least one unfamiliar discipline. Do not call development examples held-out after tuning on them.

Compare baseline and skill-assisted outputs under the same model, prompt, source access, and generation budget. Keep labels anonymous, randomize placement, and repeat pairwise comparisons with reversed order. Ask evaluators to cite textual evidence per rubric dimension and judge source support against the supplied passages. Treat inconsistent preferences as unresolved rather than quietly selecting the favorable ordering. Include subject-competent human review for claims of scholarly quality; an LLM review is a diagnostic aid.

Record observed failures, preferences, ties, access limits, and the denominator. Separate factual/citation defects from argument quality and prose quality. Include equal-content verbosity controls and known citation mismatches to test the evaluator itself. A tiny pilot supports local revision decisions, not a percentage claim of broad superiority. “World-leading” would require a defined competitor set, representative blinded human evaluation, sufficient independent cases, and reported uncertainty; none has been established here.

## Additional system research (checked 2026-09-26)

- [OpenScholar, Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10072-4): combines passage retrieval, reranking, citation-aware generation and feedback. ScholarQABench evaluates literature questions across four scientific domains. Borrow passage-grounded verification and gap-driven retrieval; its benchmark does not establish performance on humanities essays or full manuscripts. Do not transfer its reported numerical gains to Mobius.
- [STORM, NAACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.347/): researches diverse perspectives before organizing an outline. Its target is Wikipedia-like articles, not original academic contribution. Borrow a bounded perspective check; avoid expanding a short argumentative essay into an encyclopedia.
- [ScholarCopilot, COLM 2025](https://openreview.net/pdf/7ea1b2c5ff292ac8983dfe38db0674217a2fa774.pdf): integrates citation retrieval into generation, trained on computer-science papers. Borrow retrieval at an unresolved claim; a Skill alone is not equivalent to its trained retriever/model, and evidence from its discipline is not cross-domain validation.
